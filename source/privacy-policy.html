02The app has no servers

Tells is a self-contained app. It has no backend, no user database and no cloud component. It makes no network requests carrying your content, because it makes no network requests at all in the course of doing its job.

The practical proof is in your hands: put your phone in aeroplane mode and the app works exactly the same. Pick a photo, get a result, browse your history. If your photo were going anywhere, that wouldn't be possible.

03What happens to your photo

Everything, start to finish, on your device:

  • You pick a photo. iOS shows you a picker that runs outside our app, so only the single image you choose is ever handed over — the app cannot see, browse or search the rest of your library.
  • The image is analyzed locally, by your phone, using Apple's on-device vision frameworks. It measures things like the distance between two bodies, which way they're turned, whether there's contact, posture and expression.
  • The result appears. The image is released from memory.
  • A small thumbnail is saved to the app's private storage on your device so your history list has something to show. Nothing else about the photo is kept.

At no point is the photo transmitted, uploaded, backed up to us, shared, sold, published, or shown to any person at [COMPANY]. We have built no mechanism that could do any of those things.

Sharing a result is a separate, deliberate act. Nothing leaves the app unless you tap Share, and then it goes wherever you send it, through iOS. The card the app makes contains your score, the label, the one-line summary and the two bars — it never contains the photo. That is a design decision, made specifically so that nobody's picture can be posted from this app.

04What we do not collect

To be exhaustive rather than reassuring:

  • No name, email address, phone number, password, date of birth or any other account detail — there is no account.
  • No photos, no thumbnails, no analysis results, no history.
  • No contacts, calendar, microphone, health data or location. The app never requests these permissions.
  • No advertising identifier (IDFA), no device fingerprint, no persistent user ID. The app does not contain Apple's tracking framework at all, which is why it never shows you a tracking prompt.
  • No analytics SDK, no crash-reporting SDK, no attribution or advertising SDK. The app ships with no third-party code in it whatsoever — only Apple's own system frameworks.
  • No usage statistics. We do not know how often you open the app, how many analyses you've run, or what any of them said.

The app writes three things to its own private storage area on your device, readable by no other app:

Your past results — scores, write-ups, labels, dates, and any star rating you gaveSo your history works
A small thumbnail of each analyzed photoSo the history list has something to show

Settings → Delete all my checks erases all of it immediately and permanently. Deleting the app removes it too.

One thing outside our control that you should know about: if you have iPhone backups switched on, iOS may include this app's local data in your own iCloud or computer backup. That backup is yours and is governed by Apple's terms, not ours — we have no access to it. You can exclude it in your device settings.

06The two ways we could end up with something of yours

Everything above is about the app. There are exactly two situations in which data of yours could reach us, and both require you to take an action outside the app.

If you email us

If you write to our support address we will have your email address and whatever you put in the message. We use it to answer you and nothing else. We keep it for [SUPPORT_RETENTION, e.g. 12 months] and then delete it. Legal basis: our legitimate interest in answering support requests (GDPR Art. 6(1)(f)).

One thing worth being straight about if you're outside Türkiye: an email to us arrives in Türkiye, where we are. Türkiye is not covered by an EU adequacy decision, so for readers in the EEA or the UK that is a transfer to a third country. It is made under GDPR Article 49(1)(b) — a transfer necessary to take steps at your own request — and, in sending it, Article 49(1)(a). If you would prefer nothing of yours to reach Türkiye, don't email us. There is no other route by which anything of yours could get here — not even reading this page, as the next part explains.

If you open this page or the terms

These two pages are static files published through GitHub Pages. Opening one takes you out of the app and onto GitHub's servers, not ours. GitHub Pages gives a site's author no access logs and no visitor statistics, and we have added neither, so we receive no record of your visit — we cannot see that you read this, when, or from where.

GitHub itself does log the request, as any host does, to keep its service running and secure and to meet its own legal obligations. That is GitHub's processing rather than ours and it is governed by GitHub's privacy statement, which has a section specifically about visitors to GitHub Pages sites. GitHub is a company in the United States, so for readers in the EEA or the UK, opening this page reaches a US host under GitHub's own transfer arrangements. We are not in a position to promise a retention period on GitHub's behalf, so we don't claim one.

We have added nothing to these pages. No analytics, no tag manager, no trackers, no advertising, no embedded fonts, no third-party scripts, and no cookies of ours. Everything each page needs is inside the file itself, which is why they work with JavaScript switched off. We say “of ours” deliberately: what a host does at its own layer is the host's to describe, and GitHub's statement above is the place it is described.

None of this reaches the app. The app itself contains no third-party code whatsoever and sets no cookies, because it isn't a web page and talks to nobody — see sections 2 and 4. Reading this policy is a visit to a website; using Tells is not.

And one that isn't ours

Your download of the app, and any interaction with the App Store or the “rate us” prompt, is between you and Apple under Apple's privacy policy. Separately, if you have turned on Share With App Developers in your iOS settings, Apple may give us anonymised crash and performance reports. That is Apple's system, it is off unless you switched it on, you can switch it off at any time in Settings → Privacy & Security → Analytics & Improvements, and it contains no photos and no content from your analyses.

08What this app is not

We want to be unambiguous, because “an app that analyzes photos of people” could reasonably be assumed to do things it does not do.

  • We do not perform facial recognition.
  • We do not create, store, or compare faceprints, face geometry, or any other biometric template or identifier.
  • We cannot identify anyone. We have no database of faces, no way to match a face across two photos, and no way to connect a face to a name.
  • We do not detect or infer race, ethnicity, religion, health, disability, sexual orientation, or any other special category of data.

What the app measures is the arrangement of two bodies in a single frame: distance, orientation, contact, posture, expression. Because this does not allow or confirm the unique identification of anyone, it is not biometric data within the meaning of GDPR Article 4(14) and falls outside Article 9. For the same reason it is not a “biometric identifier” under laws such as the Illinois Biometric Information Privacy Act or the Texas Capture or Use of Biometric Identifier Act.

09The other person in the photo

Almost every photo you analyze has someone else in it. We'd rather address that directly than let it go unmentioned.

When you use a photo, you are responsible for having the right to do so — see the Terms of Use, which prohibit analyzing photos of children, intimate images, and photos of anyone who would object.

What protects them, structurally, is that there is nothing for us to misuse:

  • Their image never reaches us. It cannot — the app has no way to send it.
  • They are never identified, because the app cannot identify anyone.
  • Their image cannot be shared out of the app; the share card contains no photo.
  • Nothing the app produces is a claim about them as a person. It describes what two bodies are doing in one frame, and it says so on every result screen.

If you appear in a photo someone put through this app and want to contact us, write to [EMAIL]. We will not be holding a copy of the image — we never had one — and we will confirm that to you in writing.

11Your rights, wherever you are

Privacy laws differ in their detail, but they give you broadly the same set of rights: to ask whether we hold personal data about you, to get a copy, to have it corrected or erased, to restrict or object to how it's used, to take it elsewhere, and not to be subjected to significant decisions made purely by a machine.

You have all of those here, and two honest notes on what they amount to in practice:

  • There is almost never anything for us to give you or delete. We hold no account and receive no photos, results or usage data. The only record we might have is a support email you sent us — covered in section 6, and erasable on request. Your app data is erased by you, in Settings, in one tap.
  • The app makes no automated decision about you in the sense these laws mean — GDPR Article 22 and its equivalents. It produces an entertainment score. It has no legal or similarly significant effect and determines nothing about you.

To exercise any right, write to [EMAIL]. We answer within 30 days, and we don't charge for it.
