Thanks for testing Tells.

WHAT IT DOES

You give it one photo with two people in it. It reads the body language — how close you're standing, which way you're turned, whether there's contact and whether it was returned, posture, expression, where the heads are pointed — and turns that into a score with a written explanation.

It's entertainment. It is not psychology, not evidence, and not advice about anyone.

HOW TO TRY IT

Pick a photo with TWO PEOPLE, ideally waist-up rather than a tight head crop. Say which side is you, pick a context (Crush, Situationship, Just met, Friend, Ex, It's complicated), and you'll get a result in a few seconds.

Try several different photos. The interesting cases are the ones where two people are doing something specific — a hand on a shoulder, one person leaning in, both turned to the camera.

WHAT I ACTUALLY NEED FROM YOU

This is the useful part. The engine has only ever been tested on a few dozen photos, and the last round of testing found three real bugs that were invisible one photo at a time.

**When the result doesn't match the photo, tell me.** Send:

1. The original photo
2. A screenshot of the result
3. One line on what's wrong — "it said no contact but his arm is around her"

That single pairing is worth more than any amount of "it was fun". Wrong answers are the data I need; right answers I already have.

Also worth reporting:
- It refused a photo you thought was perfectly clear
- A signal says one thing and the sentence under it says another
- Any wording that reads as creepy, judgmental or just wrong

YOU WILL SEE IT REFUSE THINGS

If a photo has one person, is very tightly cropped, or is very dark, the app declines and tells you which part it couldn't read. That's deliberate — it refuses rather than inventing a score. If it refuses something it clearly shouldn't have, that's a bug and I want to know.

ABOUT YOUR PHOTOS

Nothing leaves your phone. There's no account, no server, and no analytics — the whole analysis runs on your device. Turn on airplane mode and the app still works completely.

That matters here because you'll be using photos of real people who haven't agreed to anything. Those images never reach me. Please don't analyze photos of children, intimate images, or photos of someone who'd be upset about it.

A note I mean seriously: a score is never permission. Consent comes from what a person says, not from what an app told you about a photo.

You must be 18 or older to use it.

REPORTING

Easiest way: take a screenshot inside TestFlight and it'll offer to send feedback with it. Or just message me directly — for a mismatch, please include the original photo, otherwise I can't reproduce it.
